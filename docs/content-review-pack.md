## General red flags

- A deformed, very swollen or unusable hand after an injury, or a wound over a broken bone
- A cut or puncture followed by a red, swollen finger held bent — or fever and spreading redness
- A tiny puncture from a paint, grease or pressure-washer gun — even if it barely hurts, go to emergency now
- One joint that is hot, red and swollen, especially with fever — infection can look like gout
- A cut after which you can't bend or straighten the finger, or the tip feels numb
- A cat, dog or human bite over a joint or tendon
- After an injury or in a cast: pain far worse than expected, worse when the fingers are pulled straight, with a tight, hard forearm or hand
- A cold, pale or blue finger or hand
- Sudden numbness or weakness with face droop, slurred speech or confusion — call emergency services

### Pre-ranking checklist

- Deformed, very swollen or unusable after an injury, or bone showing
- One joint hot, red and swollen, or fever
- A cut, bite or puncture — especially if the finger won't move or the tip is numb
- A puncture from a paint, grease or pressure-washer gun
- A finger or hand that is cold, pale or blue
- Pain far worse than expected after injury or in a cast, with a tight, hard forearm
- Sudden weakness or numbness with face droop, slurred speech or confusion

## Nerve areas

- **Median nerve:** Palm side of the thumb, index, middle and thumb-side half of the ring finger (plus their fingertips on the back)
- **Ulnar nerve:** Little finger and the little-finger half of the ring finger, front and back, plus that edge of the hand
- **Radial nerve:** Back of the thumb (not the nail), the back of the index and middle fingers near the knuckles, and the back of the hand on the thumb side

- **Paronychia (nail-fold infection)** — Red, swollen, tender skin beside the nail, sometimes with a pocket of pus. Often follows a hangnail or nail biting.
  - *What helps:* Warm soaks 3–4× a day for early cases; see a clinician if pus forms — it may need draining.
  - *Sources:* [StatPearls: Paronychia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544307/)
- **Crush injury or tuft fracture** — Door slams and hammer blows can fracture the fingertip bone or bleed under the nail.
  - *What helps:* Ice, elevate and protect with a padded splint. A large, painful blood blister under the nail can be released by a clinician.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Broken finger or thumb](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/broken-finger/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Hand fractures](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/hand-fractures/); [StatPearls: Phalanx fractures of the hand](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557625/); [StatPearls: Bennett fracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500035/)
- **Carpal tunnel syndrome (median nerve)** — Tingling or numb tips of the thumb, index, middle or thumb-side of the ring finger usually come from the median nerve squeezed at the wrist.
  - *What helps:* Night wrist splint, nerve glides; see the wrist (palm side) region.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/); [StatPearls: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448179/)
- **Ulnar nerve compression (elbow or wrist)** — Tingling in the little finger and the little-finger side of the ring finger points to the ulnar nerve — squeezed at the elbow (cubital tunnel) or wrist (Guyon's canal).
  - *What helps:* Avoid leaning on elbows or handlebars; keep elbows straighter at night.
  - *Sources:* [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Cubital tunnel syndrome](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/ulnar-nerve-entrapment-at-the-elbow/); [StatPearls: Ulnar nerve entrapment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK555929/)
- **Felon (fingertip pulp infection)** — Tense, throbbing, very painful swelling of the fingertip pad, often after a small puncture.
  - *What helps:* Needs prompt medical care — usually antibiotics and sometimes drainage.
  - *Sources:* [StatPearls: Felon](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK430933/)
- **Raynaud's phenomenon** — Fingertips turn white or blue in the cold or with stress, then red and throbbing as they rewarm.
  - *What helps:* Keep the whole body warm, wear gloves, avoid smoking; ask about it if it is new or one-sided.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Raynaud's](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/raynauds/); [StatPearls: Raynaud disease](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499833/)
- **Glomus tumour** — Rare, benign growth under the nail causing pinpoint pain and cold sensitivity.
  - *What helps:* Diagnosed by a hand specialist; treated by removal.
  - *Sources:* [PubMed Central: Glomus tumor: revitalizing concepts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4567371/)
- **Cut with tendon or nerve injury** — A cut on the finger after which the tip feels numb on one side, or the finger will not bend or straighten fully — the tendon or digital nerve may be cut even if the wound looks small.
  - *What helps:* Same-day assessment: tendon and nerve repairs work best early.
  - *Sources:* [StatPearls: Flexor tendon lacerations](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK493223/); [NHS: Cuts and grazes](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes/)
- **High-pressure injection injury** — A pinhole wound from a paint, grease or pressure-washer gun, often on the non-dominant index finger. It looks trivial for hours, then the finger swells and the tissue dies.
  - *What helps:* Emergency — go now, even if it barely hurts. Do not wait to see if it settles.
  - *Sources:* [StatPearls: Hand high pressure injury](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542210/)

**Tips:** Keep cuts and hangnails clean; don't pick cuticles · Warm salt-water soaks for early nail-fold infections · Protect a sore tip with a padded finger cap or tape · Warm gloves and hand warmers if cold triggers pain

**Exercises:** tendonGlides

**See a clinician soon if:** Tense, throbbing fingertip swelling or spreading redness; Fever with a painful finger; Numbness that is constant or getting worse; A puncture from a paint, grease or pressure gun — emergency; A cut after which the tip is numb or the finger won't move properly

- **Osteoarthritis (Heberden's nodes)** — Bony bumps on the end knuckles with aching and stiffness, common after 50 and often runs in families.
  - *What helps:* Warm water in the morning, gentle movement, larger-grip tools; topical anti-inflammatory gels can ease flares.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Osteoarthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/osteoarthritis/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Arthritis of the hand](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/arthritis-of-the-hand/)
- **Mallet finger** — The fingertip droops and cannot straighten on its own after being jammed (e.g. by a ball). The extensor tendon is torn or pulled off with a bone fleck.
  - *What helps:* Needs a splint holding the tip straight at all times for up to 8 weeks — get it checked promptly, ideally the same day.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Mallet finger](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/mallet-finger/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Mallet finger](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/mallet-finger-baseball-finger/)
- **Jersey finger (FDP avulsion)** — After grabbing a jersey or being yanked, the fingertip cannot bend. About 3 in 4 cases involve the ring finger.
  - *What helps:* Time-sensitive — repair within about 10 days gives the best results, so get seen promptly.
  - *Sources:* [StatPearls: Jersey finger](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK545291/)
- **Mucous (digital myxoid) cyst** — A small, smooth bump between the end knuckle and the nail, linked to arthritis. It can groove the nail.
  - *What helps:* Often left alone; don't pop it (infection risk). Can be removed if painful.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Ganglion cyst](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ganglion-cyst/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Ganglion cyst of the wrist and hand](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/ganglion-cyst-of-the-wrist-and-hand/)
- **Psoriatic arthritis** — Swollen end knuckles, a "sausage" finger or pitted nails, often with psoriasis.
  - *What helps:* See a GP / rheumatologist — early treatment protects joints.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Psoriatic arthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/psoriatic-arthritis/)
- **Gout / pseudogout** — A sudden, exquisitely tender, hot red end knuckle, sometimes on top of existing arthritic nodes — more common in older adults on water tablets.
  - *What helps:* Rest, ice and see a GP the same day if it is hot and red: infection can look identical.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Gout](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gout/); [StatPearls: Gout](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546606/)

**Tips:** Morning warm-up: soak hands in warm water, then gentle bends · Use built-up grips, jar openers and lever taps · After a jam: if the tip won't straighten, splint it straight and get checked

## Middle knuckle (PIP joint) (`pip`)

- **Jammed finger (sprain / volar plate injury)** — Ball or fall to the fingertip sprains the side ligaments or the plate on the palm side. Swelling can linger for weeks.
  - *What helps:* If the joint was dislocated, looks crooked, is very swollen, or you can't straighten the middle knuckle, get an X-ray first. Otherwise buddy-tape to the neighbour, ice, elevate and move it gently.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Sprains and strains](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/sprains-and-strains/); [StatPearls: Finger dislocation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK551508/)
- **Osteoarthritis (Bouchard's nodes)** — Bony enlargement with stiffness and aching, often alongside end-knuckle arthritis.
  - *What helps:* Heat, gentle range-of-motion, joint protection; a hand therapist can fit splints.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Osteoarthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/osteoarthritis/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Arthritis of the hand](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/arthritis-of-the-hand/)
- **Rheumatoid arthritis** — Soft, warm swelling in several finger joints on both hands with morning stiffness lasting over 30–60 minutes.
  - *What helps:* See a GP promptly — early treatment prevents joint damage.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Rheumatoid arthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rheumatoid-arthritis/)
- **Boutonnière injury (central slip)** — After a jam, the middle knuckle stays bent and cannot straighten while the tip bends back.
  - *What helps:* Needs splinting of the PIP straight — see a clinician early.
  - *Sources:* [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Boutonnière deformity](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/boutonniere-deformity/); [StatPearls: Boutonniere deformity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK470323/)
- **Dislocation or fracture** — Obvious deformity or severe pain after injury. A fracture-dislocation of this joint is often missed and is the commonest cause of a permanently stiff finger.
  - *What helps:* Don't pull it back yourself — get an X-ray.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Broken finger or thumb](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/broken-finger/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Hand fractures](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/hand-fractures/); [StatPearls: Phalanx fractures of the hand](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557625/); [StatPearls: Bennett fracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500035/)
- **Psoriatic arthritis (dactylitis)** — A whole finger swollen like a sausage, nail pitting, or psoriasis anywhere on the skin.
  - *What helps:* See a GP / rheumatologist — early treatment protects the joints.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Psoriatic arthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/psoriatic-arthritis/)
- **Gout / pseudogout** — A sudden hot, red, exquisitely tender joint, often overnight.
  - *What helps:* Rest, ice and same-day GP review if hot and red — infection can look the same.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Gout](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gout/); [StatPearls: Gout](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546606/)

**Tips:** Buddy-tape a sprained finger to its neighbour for 2–3 weeks · Ice 10–15 minutes and keep the hand above heart level early on · Once a clinician has ruled out a fracture or central slip injury: gently bend and straighten several times a day as pain allows · Rings off early if the finger is swelling

- **Trigger finger (stenosing tenosynovitis)** — The finger catches, clicks or locks when bending, with a tender nodule at the base in the palm. Worse in the morning; more common with diabetes.
  - *What helps:* Rest from hard gripping, a night splint holding the finger straight, gentle tendon glides. A steroid injection often settles it.

- **Flexor tendinitis from gripping** — Aching at the finger base after heavy gripping, climbing or tool use.
  - *What helps:* Reduce grip load, use padded or wider handles, rebuild gradually.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Repetitive strain injury](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/repetitive-strain-injury-rsi/); [NHS: Tendonitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/tendonitis/)
- **Dupuytren's nodule** — A firm, usually painless lump in the palm that may form a cord and slowly pull the finger bent (ring and little fingers most often).
  - *What helps:* Monitor with the tabletop test (can you lay your hand flat?). Treatments exist if it contracts.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Dupuytren's contracture](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/dupuytrens-contracture/); [StatPearls: Dupuytren contracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526074/)
- **Retinacular (pulley) ganglion** — A small, firm, pea-sized cyst at the finger base, painful when gripping.
  - *What helps:* Often settles on its own; can be aspirated if bothersome.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Ganglion cyst](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/ganglion-cyst/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Ganglion cyst of the wrist and hand](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/ganglion-cyst-of-the-wrist-and-hand/)
- **Flexor sheath infection** — After a cut or puncture: an evenly swollen finger, held slightly bent, very painful to straighten.
  - *What helps:* Emergency — needs urgent antibiotics and often surgery.
  - *Sources:* [StatPearls: Pyogenic flexor tenosynovitis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK576414/)
- **Psoriatic arthritis (dactylitis)** — The whole finger swollen like a sausage, with nail pitting or psoriasis.
  - *What helps:* See a GP / rheumatologist.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Psoriatic arthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/psoriatic-arthritis/)
- **Cut with tendon or nerve injury** — A cut in the palm or finger base after which the finger won't bend fully or one side of it is numb.

## Knuckle (MCP joint) (`knuckle`)

The big knuckles take the force of punching, gripping and pushing. Several arthritis types start here.

**Structures:** Metacarpal head, Knuckle cartilage, Extensor tendon, Sagittal bands, Interosseous muscles

- **Boxer's fracture** — Break of the metacarpal neck (usually little or ring finger) after punching; the knuckle looks sunken.
  - *What helps:* Ice and elevate; needs an X-ray. Check the finger doesn't rotate when you make a fist.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Broken finger or thumb](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/broken-finger/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Hand fractures](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/hand-fractures/); [StatPearls: Phalanx fractures of the hand](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK557625/); [StatPearls: Bennett fracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500035/)
- **Rheumatoid arthritis** — Knuckles on both hands swollen and stiff, especially in the morning.
  - *What helps:* See a GP — blood tests and early treatment make a big difference.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Rheumatoid arthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rheumatoid-arthritis/)
- **Sagittal band injury ("boxer's knuckle")** — The extensor tendon slips off the top of the knuckle when making a fist, with painful snapping.
  - *What helps:* Splinting in a specific position; see a hand therapist.
  - *Sources:* [PubMed Central: Sagittal band, boutonniere, and pulley injuries in the athlete](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5344850/)
- **Osteoarthritis** — Aching, stiff knuckles — uncommon here without an old injury; if several knuckles are involved, ask about iron levels.
  - *What helps:* Heat for stiffness, gentle movement, larger grips.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Osteoarthritis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/osteoarthritis/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Arthritis of the hand](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/arthritis-of-the-hand/)
- **Gout / pseudogout** — A single knuckle that becomes hot, red and exquisitely tender over hours.
  - *What helps:* Rest and ice; same-day GP review if hot and red — infection can look identical.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Gout](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/gout/); [StatPearls: Gout](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK546606/)
- **Fight bite** — A tooth cut over the knuckle from a punch — high infection risk even if small.
  - *What helps:* Urgent medical care and antibiotics.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Animal and human bites](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/animal-and-human-bites/)

**Tips:** Ice and elevate after an impact · Avoid heavy gripping or push-ups on the knuckles while sore · Warm up stiff knuckles with gentle fist-and-open cycles

**Exercises:** tendonGlides, fingerSpreads

**See a clinician soon if:** A skin break over the knuckle from a punch; Finger crosses over its neighbour when you make a fist; Hot, red swelling

  - *Sources:* [NHS: Mallet finger](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/mallet-finger/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Mallet finger](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/mallet-finger-baseball-finger/)
- **EPL tendon rupture** — Suddenly can't lift the thumb tip, often weeks after a wrist fracture or with rheumatoid arthritis — no jam needed.
  - *What helps:* Not a splint problem — see a hand surgeon soon; the tendon ends retract.
  - *Sources:* [PubMed Central: Spontaneous atraumatic extensor pollicis longus rupture](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3587012/)
- **Raynaud's phenomenon** — Thumb turns white or blue in the cold. Thumb involvement is less common and more often suggests an underlying cause.
  - *What helps:* Keep warm; mention thumb involvement to a clinician.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Raynaud's](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/raynauds/); [StatPearls: Raynaud disease](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK499833/)
- **Paronychia** — Infection beside the nail.
  - *What helps:* Warm soaks; see a clinician if pus forms.
  - *Sources:* [StatPearls: Paronychia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544307/)
- **Carpal tunnel syndrome** — Tingling in the thumb tip (with index and middle) from the median nerve.
  - *What helps:* Night wrist splint, nerve glides — see the wrist (palm side) region.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/); [StatPearls: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448179/)

**Tips:** Use pens and tools with fatter grips · Split heavy pinching tasks into shorter bouts · Warm soaks for stiffness

**Exercises:** thumbOpposition, tendonGlides

**See a clinician soon if:** Suddenly unable to lift the thumb; Spreading redness or fever

## Thumb pad (thenar eminence) (`thenar`)

Three small muscles move the thumb. They are powered by a branch of the median nerve — so carpal tunnel can weaken or shrink them.

**Structures:** Thenar muscles, Recurrent branch of median nerve, Flexor pollicis longus, Transverse carpal ligament

| Bruise / contusion | `contusion` | Started after an injury |  |  |  |  |

- **Carpal tunnel syndrome** — Weakness or clumsiness of the thumb and, in advanced cases, a hollowed pad. The tingling itself is felt in the fingers, not the pad — the pad's skin nerve bypasses the carpal tunnel.
  - *What helps:* Neutral wrist splint at night, nerve glides, fewer sustained wrist bends.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/carpal-tunnel-syndrome/); [StatPearls: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448179/)
- **Muscle overuse (texting, gaming, tools)** — Aching thumb pad after long bouts of thumb work.
  - *What helps:* Breaks, switch hands, self-massage and gentle stretching.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Repetitive strain injury](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/repetitive-strain-injury-rsi/); [NHS: Tendonitis](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/tendonitis/)
- **Basal thumb arthritis (referred ache)** — Arthritis at the thumb base often aches into the pad.
  - *What helps:* See the thumb base region.
  - *Sources:* [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Arthritis of the thumb](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/arthritis-of-the-thumb/)
- **Bruise / contusion** — Direct blow to the pad.
  - *What helps:* Ice, protect, resolves in 1–2 weeks.
  - *Sources:* [NHS: Hand pain](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/hand-pain/)

**Tips:** Wear a neutral wrist splint at night if tingling wakes you · Break up long phone or controller sessions · Gently massage the thumb pad in circles

**Exercises:** medianNerveGlide, thumbOpposition

## Self-checks

### Eichhoff test (home Finkelstein) (`finkelstein`)

- **For:** De Quervain's tenosynovitis
- **How:** Tuck your thumb into your palm, close your fingers over it, then gently tilt your wrist toward your little finger.
- **Positive if:** Sharp pain on the thumb side of the wrist
- **Reliability note:** Often uncomfortable even in healthy wrists, so compare with your other hand. The true Finkelstein test is done by a clinician who moves the wrist for you.
- **Weights:** yes +2, no −1; conditions: De Quervain's tenosynovitis ×1, Intersection syndrome ×0.3
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** yes
- **Caution:** Stop as soon as it hurts — no bouncing.
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: De Quervain tenosynovitis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK442005/)

### Phalen's test (`phalen`)

- **For:** Carpal tunnel syndrome
- **How:** Let your wrists hang fully bent down, backs of the hands pressed together, for up to 60 seconds.
- **Positive if:** Tingling or numbness in the thumb, index or middle finger
- **Reliability note:** In studies it picks up about 68% of carpal tunnel cases and is correctly negative about 73% of the time — it supports the diagnosis but can't confirm or rule it out.
- **Weights:** yes +1.8, no −0.6; conditions: Carpal tunnel syndrome ×1
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** no
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448179/)

### Tinel's sign at the wrist (`tinel`)

- **For:** Carpal tunnel syndrome
- **How:** Tap firmly 4–6 times with two fingertips over the middle of the palm-side wrist crease.
- **Positive if:** An electric tingle shooting into the thumb, index or middle finger
- **Reliability note:** Picks up only about half of carpal tunnel cases (specificity about 77%), so a negative result doesn't rule it out. Normally done by a clinician.
- **Weights:** yes +1.5, no −0.4; conditions: Carpal tunnel syndrome ×1
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** no
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: Carpal tunnel syndrome](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448179/)

### Thumb base grind test (`grind`)

- **For:** Basal thumb arthritis
- **How:** Hold the thumb's knuckle with your other hand, gently push the thumb toward its base and rotate it in small circles.
- **Positive if:** Deep aching or a grinding feeling at the base of the thumb
- **Reliability note:** A common clinical sign, but pain can also come from nearby tendons; X-rays confirm arthritis.
- **Weights:** yes +2.2, no −0.8; conditions: Basal thumb (CMC) arthritis ×1
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** yes
- **Sources:** [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Arthritis of the thumb](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/arthritis-of-the-thumb/)

### Tabletop test (`tabletop`)

- **For:** Dupuytren's disease
- **How:** Place your hand palm-down on a table and try to lay the palm and all the fingers completely flat.
- **Positive if:** You can't get the palm and fingers flat
- **Reliability note:** Used to decide when a Dupuytren's contracture is worth treating; early nodules can be present with a normal test.
- **Weights:** yes +2.6, no −0.8; conditions: Dupuytren's disease ×1
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** no
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: Dupuytren contracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526074/)

### Fist-and-release (`fistOpen`)

- **For:** Trigger finger
- **How:** Make a firm fist, then open the hand quickly. Repeat five times.
- **Positive if:** A finger catches, clicks or locks, with a tender spot at its base
- **Reliability note:** Triggering is diagnosed mainly from this history and a tender nodule at the finger base; it may not happen every time.
- **Weights:** yes +2.6, no −0.5; conditions: Trigger finger / trigger thumb ×1
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** yes
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: Trigger finger](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK459310/)

### Finger-cross test (`cross`)

- **For:** Ulnar nerve weakness
- **How:** Cross your middle finger over your index finger, uncross, and repeat quickly. Compare with the other hand.
- **Positive if:** Clumsy, weak or unable to cross compared with the other hand
- **Reliability note:** Tests the small hand muscles powered by the ulnar nerve; weakness usually appears later than numbness.
- **Weights:** yes +1.6, no −0.6; conditions: Cubital tunnel syndrome (ulnar nerve at the elbow) ×1, Ulnar nerve compression at the wrist (Guyon's canal) ×1
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** no
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: Ulnar nerve entrapment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK555929/)

### Press-up test (`pressUp`)

- **For:** TFCC tear / ulnar-side wrist pain
- **How:** Sitting in a chair with armrests, push yourself up with your hands flat on the armrests.
- **Positive if:** Pain on the little-finger side of the wrist
- **Reliability note:** A simple screening sign for ulnar-sided wrist problems; it can't tell a TFCC tear from ulnar impaction — that needs examination and imaging.
- **Weights:** yes +1.8, no −0.6; conditions: TFCC tear ×1, Ulnar impaction syndrome ×0.6, Extensor tendinitis / dorsal wrist impingement ×0.3
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** yes
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: Triangular fibrocartilage complex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK554564/)

### Snuffbox press (`snuffbox`)

- **For:** Scaphoid fracture
- **How:** Lift your thumb to show the hollow on the thumb side of the wrist, then press into that hollow with a fingertip.
- **Positive if:** Sharp, pinpoint pain — especially after a fall
- **Reliability note:** Very sensitive but not specific — many sprains are tender here too. That's why tenderness after a fall is treated as a possible fracture until imaging says otherwise.
- **Weights:** yes +2.4, no −1; conditions: Scaphoid fracture ×1
- **Loads the area (withheld after injury):** no
- **Caution:** If this hurts after a fall, get an X-ray even if the injury seemed minor.
- **Sources:** [StatPearls: Scaphoid wrist fracture](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536907/); [OrthoInfo (AAOS): Scaphoid fracture of the wrist](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/scaphoid-fracture-of-the-wrist/)
